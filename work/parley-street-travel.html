Fifty seats, twenty payment links.

A booking site for a five-day history tour: 20 Stripe payment links across five room types, a deposit-and-plan schedule, and a help panel that answers from the page itself.

Role
Design and front-end build
Year
2026
Scope
Brand application, site, Stripe booking
Status
Live

The brief

Parley Street Travel Co. is a new tour company, and The Witnesses Tour is its first trip: five days through Missouri and Nauvoo, fifty seats on one coach, departing April 2027. They needed a site that could actually sell seats, not a brochure that sent people to a phone number.

That turns a one-page site into a commerce problem. A seat is not a product with one price. It costs a different amount depending on the room you take, it can be paid as a deposit or in full, and the rest can be settled later or spread across four payments.

Twenty links, five rooms

The booking runs on Stripe payment links rather than a cart, because the company is one person and a cart needs maintaining. Five room types, each with a deposit link, a pay-in-full link, a balance link and a payment-plan link: twenty links in all, driven from one config file.

Every room books a fixed quantity, so a Double always books two seats and a Quad four. Solo travelers get their own choice: keep the room or share it at the shared rate, paired afterwards.

The whole booking state lives in a single file. Opening a room, closing it, changing the deposit or marking the tour sold out is one edit, which matters when the person making the change is the owner rather than a developer.

The part that is not a link

A deposit creates an obligation three months out, and that is where the real work sits. A webhook verifies Stripe's signature and schedules the remaining payments as draft invoices. If someone pays the balance themselves or starts a plan early, the scheduled charges are deleted rather than left to fire. If a plan payment arrives on a new card, the remaining charges move to it.

None of that is visible on the page, which is the point. The visible result is that a traveler pays $250 and nothing goes wrong afterwards.

Answering questions without a chatbot

Tours generate the same handful of questions, and the obvious answer in 2026 is an AI chat panel. This one has no AI and no server. It matches keywords against the page's own FAQ and sections and returns the answer already written there.

It cannot hallucinate a refund policy, it costs nothing to run, and when it cannot match a question it offers an email address instead of guessing. It prompts once per visitor and remembers that it did.

Design

The palette is olive and cream with rust and burnt orange kept back for the sun in the logo and small accents, set in Cormorant Garamond and Jost. The header image is an 1854 wood engraving of the Mississippi crossing, public domain, recolored and faded so type sits over it.

Image licensing was a real constraint rather than a detail. Church-owned photography bars commercial use, so every image is public domain or Creative Commons cleared for commercial use, with the attribution line in the footer kept in step with whatever is on the page.

Where it stands

Live at parleystreettravel.com since October 2026 and selling. Eighty-four commits between October and now, all of it hand-written HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript with no build step, deploying from GitHub on every push.
