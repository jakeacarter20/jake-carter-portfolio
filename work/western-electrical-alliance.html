A contractor site that had to speak for every branch.

A self-directed build. I set myself a real-world brief, a Northern Utah electrical contractor with six service lines, and designed and coded the whole site to see it through.

Role
Design and front-end build
Year
2025
Scope
Website, six sections, project gallery
Status
Self-directed

The brief

Nobody hired me for this. I wanted a project that would push both halves of what I do, design and front-end, against a brief with real constraints rather than an invented one, so I took an electrical contractor: family-built since 2016, three branches in Orem, Logan and Ogden.

The hard part is that six very different service lines, from fire alarm to power metering, all have to sit on one site without turning the homepage into a directory. And the site has one job: get a commercial project manager to a contact form quickly, with enough proof on the way there to bother filling it in.

What I did

I structured the site around six sections that follow how a buyer actually reads: who the company is, what it does, where it operates, proof of work, who they would deal with, and how to start a conversation.

The project gallery carries its own pages per job, so a substation build and a student housing complex each get room to be shown properly rather than sharing one grid.

Everything is hand-built in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with a slab serif and a working grotesk carrying the industrial weight the sector calls for. It is responsive, keyboard navigable and has a skip link.

Outcome

Three branches, six service lines and four gallery case pages sitting inside one site that still opens with a single clear statement.

There are no traffic numbers to quote, because this was never deployed for a client. What it does show is a complete site taken from brief to working code by one person. View the live build.
